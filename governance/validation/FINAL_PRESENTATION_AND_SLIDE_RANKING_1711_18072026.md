## Methodology

Scores use a 0–10 scale. The overall score applies the Atlas claim-first weighting:

- Claim content and canonical alignment: **45%**
- Visual quality: **20%**
- Readability and scanability: **15%**
- Sales focus and persuasiveness: **20%**

This weighting preserves the existing Atlas requirement that claim accuracy and canonical alignment dominate the ranking. Sales strength cannot compensate for unsupported pricing, provider, entitlement, Family, legal, privacy or availability claims.

The ranking is an editorial/design assessment, not a release approval. Current `PENDING_REVIEW` gates remain unchanged.

### 1. Platform lanes v3

**Why it ranks first:** The deck has the strongest claim discipline, the clearest consultative logic and the best differentiation of Atlas as a governed solution layer rather than a model reseller. The five-question diagnostic, provider-lane criteria and no-permanent-model principle form a coherent B2B sales narrative. The principal weakness is visual incompleteness where source-tagged image placeholders remain.

### 2. Resources v3

**Why it ranks second:** The deck is highly credible, readable and useful across all audiences. It demonstrates expertise without overclaiming and builds trust through practical methodology. It is less commercially forceful because most assets are still labelled as planned and several slides lack a direct CTA or proof that the resources are live.

### 3. Consumer v3

**Why it ranks third:** This deck has the strongest everyday-benefit storytelling and excellent audience accessibility. Its hero and use-case slide are among the best sales slides in the set. The overall rank is reduced by the unresolved Family model, proposed prices, provider functionality and entitlement/privacy dependencies.

### 4. Prices v3

**Why it ranks fourth:** The deck has strong conversion mechanics, particularly the FAQ and closing slide, and it explains price logic clearly. It carries the greatest concentration of approval-gated claims: proposed prices, MVA treatment, provider functionality, usage mechanics, Family rules and checkout status. The six-column comparison table is also the least readable slide in the set.

## Cross-deck findings

### Claim content

Platform lanes v3 is the strongest because it consistently describes capability classes, selection criteria and approval conditions instead of promising permanent models or automatic provider access. Resources v3 is also highly claim-safe, but its availability is mostly future-state. Consumer v3 and Prices v3 contain the highest commercial value but also the greatest dependency on unresolved Family, pricing, provider and entitlement decisions.

### Visuals

All decks are structurally consistent and technically clean. The strongest card/process compositions are Platform slides 2, 6 and 7; Consumer slide 4; Prices slide 6; and Resources slides 3 and 4. The principal visual limitation across the set is the use of editable source-tagged placeholders instead of final imagery.

### Readability

The revised 150% box line spacing and content-fit sizing materially improved the set. The weakest readability points are Prices slide 8 and Resources slide 7 due to information density. Tables and long validation notices remain the main sources of cognitive load.

### Sales focus

The best direct conversion slides are Prices slide 9, Consumer slides 1–2 and Platform slide 8. Resources is the least sales-focused deck because it primarily establishes expertise and responsible-use authority while the resources remain planned.

## Recommended priority order

1. **Use Platform lanes v3 as the strongest B2B authority and solution-selection deck.**
2. **Use Consumer v3 as the strongest benefit-led audience deck after Family/provider gates are resolved.**
3. **Use Prices v3 as a conversion deck only after commercial and entitlement approval; until then, retain early-access qualification.**
4. **Use Resources v3 as a trust and expertise deck, then raise its sales score by publishing verified resources and adding outcome-led CTAs.**

## Unresolved gates

The ranking does not approve:

- proposed prices or MVA treatment;
- Family entitlement, age, guardian, privacy, membership or billing mechanics;
- provider delivery rights, API/license basis, processor roles, availability or cost;
- usage measurement, caps, reset, upgrade or billing behaviour;
- legal, privacy, accessibility or contractual claims;
- planned-resource publication or live links;
- public-production availability.
