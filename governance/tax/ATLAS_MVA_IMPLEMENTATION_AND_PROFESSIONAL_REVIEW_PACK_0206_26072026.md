## 5. Bundle/principal-supply decision framework

For every offer, document:

1. What the customer principally seeks to obtain.
2. Whether components are independently usable and separately priced.
3. Whether onboarding/setup merely enables the main digital service.
4. Whether human consultancy is substantial and separately deliverable.
5. Whether provider/API cost is an internal input or separately supplied item.
6. Whether support is ancillary to the principal service.
7. Whether different place-of-supply or exemption rules could apply.
8. Whether one or multiple tax points are created.
9. Whether invoice lines should be combined or separated.
10. Whether refunds can be allocated consistently.

### Working bundle recommendations

- Consumer subscriptions: one principal continuing digital service; allowances and routine support are components of that supply.
- Consumer top-ups: additional entitlement associated with the same service, subject to separate tax-point analysis.
- Fixed business assessment/sprint: professional service.
- Setup plus managed service: likely separate setup and recurring components where each has independent delivery and price, subject to adviser confirmation.
- Provider costs: normally Atlas's own input cost unless the contract establishes a genuine separate recharge or agency/disbursement relationship.

## 8. Top-up and premium-boost tax treatment

### 8.1 Recommended product design

To reduce ambiguity, top-ups should be:

- fixed quantities of Atlas service entitlement;
- usable only for specified Atlas services;
- non-transferable;
- non-refundable for cash except where law requires refund;
- not exchangeable for unrelated goods/services;
- attached to one customer/Family entitlement account;
- separately ordered and priced;
- supported by an entitlement ledger.

### 8.2 Tax questions requiring sign-off

- Is the top-up an advance payment for an identified service?
- Is the tax rate and place of supply known at purchase?
- Could it qualify as a single-purpose or multi-purpose voucher under applicable rules?
- When does output tax arise: purchase, redemption or another event?
- How is partial consumption valued after withdrawal/termination?
- How are promotional credits distinguished from paid entitlement?
- How does base-plan cancellation affect unused paid units?
- What credit-note correction is required?

Until answered, top-up checkout remains disabled.

## 11. Provider cost, recharge and disbursement decision tree

1. **Did Atlas contract for and consume the provider service in its own name?**  
   Default: Atlas own cost/input to its supply.
2. **Is the customer contractually buying a separately identified provider service from Atlas?**  
   Analyse taxable recharge/separate supply.
3. **Did Atlas act only as disclosed agent, with the customer as principal and original invoice attributable to the customer?**  
   Analyse genuine disbursement/agency treatment.
4. **Is the cost merely itemised for transparency?**  
   Itemisation alone does not create a disbursement.
5. **Does a markup apply?**  
   Supports own-supply/recharge analysis rather than pure disbursement.
6. **Can the amount be traced to one customer and original third-party liability?**  
   Required for any genuine disbursement position.

No public copy may state that API/provider costs are passed through without a signed tax/contract position.
